Motor insurance is the cover most Kenyans buy first and understand least. Here is what each part of a Kenyan motor policy actually does, and what decides whether your claim is paid.

## The two families of cover

**Third party only** is the legal minimum under the Traffic Act. It pays for injury and property damage you cause to other people. It does not repair your own car, and it does not pay you out if your car is stolen.

**Comprehensive** adds damage to your own vehicle, theft, and fire. Most private car owners we place cover comprehensive because a single unrepaired accident costs more than several years of premium.

There is a middle option in the market called **third party, fire and theft**. It covers your car if it burns or is stolen, but not if you hit a wall.

## What actually affects your premium

| Factor | Why the insurer cares |
| --- | --- |
| Sum insured (vehicle value) | Sets the maximum the insurer can pay for your car |
| Vehicle age and model | Parts availability and theft risk |
| Declared use | Private, commercial, PSV, or hire. Commercial use costs more |
| Overnight parking | A locked compound costs less than street parking |
| Claims history | A clean record earns a no claim discount |
| Driver age and experience | Younger drivers attract a higher rate |
| Voluntary excess | A higher excess you accept lowers the premium |

## The excess, explained without jargon

The excess is the part of each claim you pay yourself. Kenyan comprehensive policies commonly set it at five percent of the claim with a minimum of around KES 25,000.

Two practical points:

- If the repair is smaller than the excess, you pay it yourself anyway, so small scratches are usually cheaper to fix privately than to claim.
- **Excess protector** is an optional extension that eliminates the excess on a first claim, and it is worth asking about if you drive in heavy Nairobi traffic daily.

## The no claim discount

Drive claim free and most insurers reward you at renewal with a discount, typically starting around 10 percent and rising with each claim free year to a cap of 40 or 50 percent.

Claim once and the discount resets. That is why for a small repair the sensible decision is often to pay cash and keep the record clean.

## Documents that decide your claim

The insurer’s decision usually turns on paperwork that has nothing to do with fault. Have these ready:

1. **Police abstract** for any accident involving injury, a third party, theft, or damage to property
2. **Photographs** of all vehicles, the number plates, and the scene
3. **Copy of your driving licence** and the vehicle’s logbook
4. **Completed claim form** signed by the insured
5. **Repair estimate** from the approved garage, if the assessor asks for one

Failing to obtain a police abstract within a reasonable time is one of the most common reasons a legitimate claim is delayed.

## Common misunderstandings

**"My premium is cheaper so my cover is the same."** It is not. Two policies at different prices can differ on excess, on the garage network, on whether political violence is included, and on whether passengers are covered.

**"The insurer must pay whatever the garage quotes."** The insurer settles on the assessed amount, which follows the market value and the policy terms.

**"Comprehensive covers everything."** Wear and tear, mechanical breakdown, tyres damaged on their own, and driving without a licence are all excluded from every Kenyan motor policy we place.

**"A tracked car is always cheaper."** Sometimes. Some insurers require tracking above a value threshold, and some give a discount. We confirm before you buy the device.
