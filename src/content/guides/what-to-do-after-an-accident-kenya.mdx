Nothing about an accident is convenient. What you do in the first hour, however, decides whether the claim is settled in three weeks or argued about for eight months.

## At the scene

1. **Stop and check for injuries.** Medical help first, always.
2. **Make the scene safe.** Hazard lights, triangles, warning other traffic.
3. **Do not admit liability.** Not to the other driver, not in writing, not in a WhatsApp message. Fault is decided later from the evidence, and an admission you made while shaken is difficult to undo.
4. **Do not agree a private settlement at the roadside.** Even a signed handwritten agreement can complicate the claim.
5. **Call the police.** For any accident involving injury, a third party, or a disagreement about what happened, an officer must attend.

## Photograph everything before the vehicles move

Your phone camera is the most valuable claims tool you own. Capture:

- Wide shots of the scene showing both vehicles in relation to the road
- Close shots of the damage on every vehicle
- Number plates of all vehicles involved
- The road surface, skid marks, signs, and traffic lights
- Any injuries, and any witness nearby

Take more than you think you need. Storage is cheaper than a disputed claim.

## Collect the other driver’s details

- Full name and phone number
- Vehicle registration number
- Insurance company and policy number
- Driving licence number
- Names and contacts of any witnesses

If the other driver refuses to share details, note the registration and let the police handle it.

## Report to your insurer, through us

Call or WhatsApp the broker within 48 hours. We open the claim with your insurer the same working day, which matters because late notification is itself a valid reason for an insurer to reduce or decline a claim.

What we need from you at that point:

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Policy or certificate number | Identifies the cover |
| Date, time, and location | Places the incident inside the policy period |
| Police abstract | The formal record. Required for third party and theft claims |
| Photographs | Evidence of the damage and the circumstances |
| Third party details | So the insurer can write to the other insurer |
| Copy of your driving licence | Confirms the driver was licensed |

## If the vehicle must be repaired

Do not start repairs before the assessor has seen the vehicle, unless the repairs are emergency work to prevent further damage. Repairs carried out without prior approval are commonly declined.

The usual sequence is: assessment, approval of the estimate, repair at the approved garage, reinspection, settlement directly with the garage.

## If the other driver is at fault

Your insurer may settle your claim and then recover the cost from the other insurer. That subrogation process takes time, and it does not require you to negotiate with the other driver at all.

## If you are injured

A personal accident or WIBA claim runs alongside the motor claim. Keep every medical report, receipt, and the doctor’s prognosis. If you are off work, keep the documentation of lost income.

## Red flags that delay claims

- Reporting weeks after the incident
- Repairs done before assessment
- No police abstract, or an abstract that contradicts your account
- Damage that does not match the description of the accident
- A driver who was not declared on the policy

None of these are fatal on their own. All of them cost time.
